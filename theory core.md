Both [[defuse]] and [[deter]] rely on a common mathematical representation of contact events: possible interactions or communication channels that can be observed between 2 endpoints. An endpoint need not represent an identifiable (in some sense real-world) object or part of it, it is only needed to complete the definition of a contact event. Colloquially understood objects furthermore, living beings can be defined [[later]] ambiguously by "collapsing a set of endpoints". Requirements for contact events:
- $\ell:V^2=\{$endpoints $v\}^2\rightarrow\{$vectors $\phi\}$, a Hilbert space over the field $\mathbb K$; the requirement on $\phi$ to belong to such a set is somewhat arbitrary -- it is inspired by the frequent use of such objects in describing concrete examples of contact events (see examples [[later]])
- $\forall (v_1,v_2)\in V^2, \phi\in$Codom$\ell:\exists \Lambda:  (v_2,\phi,v_1)\mapsto\lambda\in\mathbb K$ and $\Lambda$ is continuous; this again seems an arbitrary restriction -- this may be lifted on a revision.
For now, note that fixing $(v_1,v_2),\Lambda$ is a continuous functional of $\phi$, and by the Riesz representation theorem, can be written as an inner product of $\phi$ and another vector. There is some freedom in choosing the inner product as well as the dependence on the endpoints, and a possibility to highlight is that $v_1^Tv_2$ and $\phi$ could be operators and the inner product could be a Hilbert-Schmidt product tr$(v_1v_2^T\phi)=\braket{v_2,\phi v_2}=(v_2\phi v_1)$ with a symbolic notation. We may favor using this specific inner product, and the symbolic notation is intended to remind us of this, but this notation, strictly speaking, represents the evaluation of $\Lambda$ with any choice.

Implicit in the above suggestion is that the endpoints belong to vector spaces themselves. This also hints at the idea that the set of endpoints and contact events as vertices and edges, respectively, can be represented by quivers.
