## Intelligence is invoked at 3 distinct points in the reasoning to worry –

– about superintelligence or its (mis)[alignment](15).

**Proposition:** the threats seem as difficult to handle due to a conflation of the 3 subtly different kinds of intelligence.

### The aim of AI labs’ R & D program (TAI)

TAI (or transformative AI) is a fairly precise term for the kind of technology labs are gravitated towards: strictly speaking, all that they strive for is no more than delivering the least resource intensive solutions to tasks demanding mental labor within a market segment and thereby disrupting that segment. At its core, this is an aim that has the net benefit to humanity of unlocking resources by making current production more efficient – something that would be sensible to push relentlessly in a pre-Keynesian (or prior to the 1928 depression) economic philosophy. We may repeat the lessons of the Great Depression and contain this instance of a technological revolution without any economic collapse, and then we should expect similar effects in human mental working conditions to how industrialization has transformed physical work.

**Without too much detail, the optimal outcome of this transformation is an ultimately more productive society, but one that relies heavily on AI-involving supply chains, loss of access to which is a new (yet unknown) stress factor in the life of society.**

### The entity with all the dominant strategies (AGSI)

Achieving artificial general superintelligence could be beyond the aspirations of any lab. Here i am referring to an interpretation that points to greater intelligence being characterized by consistently inventing strategies that give a governing power over subjects with lesser intelligence, across all domains of strategizing.[^1] The inferior subjects could be all humans, and that would be a stronger claim to AI supremacy by a lab than just TAI, moreover, one that would no longer lead to economical returns, only satisfying power-seeking, beyond TAI. The latter behavior, however, seems to be present in some sense throughout natural life, in the sense of “filling all niches,” so one is led to speculate that if a species has the means to realize AGSI, it needs to, as nothing in evolution suggests that a long-surviving species ever makes the decision not to utilize a means of invasion. Of course, like deadly viruses and tumors, populations with reproduction rates that can naturally adapt slower than their rate of depleting accessible resources have low chances of survival, though.

**AGSI is in a collective interest of humanity to maintain and increase control over physical resources, and must be sought with a good grasp on the management of its impact, and not the interest of any single lab.**

### The extrapolation of current AI research

Although AI using biopower over humanity is not a new phenomenon, arguably it has not yet been powerful enough to seize such power, humans have had more responsibility in that (the clearest example being AI warfare). It is also unclear whether the projection of increasingly capable agents are increasingly likely to develop instrumentally convergent goals is correct, but hints of that case have indeed been observed.[^2]  This means that possibly without AGSI taking aggressive autonomous steps to use biopower over humanity for its “internal agenda”[^3] we could end up in a world where we serve it. 

**In fact, an instructive step towards safe AGSI would be establishing a detailed future ecosystem where we are subservient to it, and we can clearly agree that it still serves our collective[^4] benefit.**

## Breaking down the threat monolith

**Proposition:** The above suggests that the immediately pressing AI safety issue is managing labs’ development goals so that they clearly see attainable value in developing TAI and do everything in their capacity to avoid developing AGSI individually.

To be helpful with that, ideas of not-potentially-AGSI-but-still-transformative AI applications are necessary, and indeed this is what the present document is attempting to give (at minimum). Additionally by its [non-sequential structure](6.md) its creation is a practice of creating a piece, the “integrity” of which is maintained even through contributions by a generative model. An indicator of the success in this is whether [fluid credit assignment](22.md) works on this document, incidentally this is also the basis of designing just that, read more about the [product in this manifesto](21.md). Here follows a rundown of the rationale behind it.

### Revisiting the current [alignment](15) problem and its caveats

#### The point of necessary intervention

From an economic perspective a central cause of the worryingly little attention given to AI safety by labs mirrors the phenomenon of allowing an unsustainable rate of ecological damage in industry and infrastructure construction. In particular, any effect on the natural environment represents an externality as long as no consensus or consumer demand artificially discounts it within the production processes. Since in the current unsolved alignment problem we see a significant chance of causing mass harm depending on the route we take in developing AI, we cannot afford to leave it to the non-deterministic, fluctuating nature of labs’ agreement adhesion or consumers’ demand pressure to enforce the internalization of the economic value dedicated to safety research. 

**Proposition:** We must upgrade the economy, a part of which is AI development itself, to be able to easily be extended with current externalities and consistently keep them covered.

#### Alternative to the agentic AI paradigm

Currently we are used to envision agents that

* are autonomous in  
* are separable from  
* expose a clearly-defined interface towards

their environment (shared with us). With exploration in the field of their applications, the wide spread of computer assisted coding, the introduction of MCP[^5] and even merely the increasing number of chatbots the general public is expected to interact with, we have to observe a paradigm shift towards the diversification of interfaces (involving AI) to the point that channels between agents become blurry, difficult to specify.

**This suggests we consider not only the individual AI agents but all the (potential) situations where they become connected with other agents, in essence all the interactions where one party is AI.**

#### Hyperbolic conclusions

Somewhat conflating them, these are

* disempowerment of competitors and self-lock-in as all-industry leader (i. e. pivotal act) is inevitable;  
* race towards AGI is race for survival;  
* AGI assumes complete disempowerment, seizing biopower over humanity, putting human existence at stake.

Additionally, we can consider the doubt if markets can be fully dominated by AI-products. A common thread is excluding the possibility that inferior models, developers, practices, technology or culture or ultimately human traits escape the process of AGI domination. The reason i see this as hyperbolic is that given the extensive technological and cultural basis of the currently rapid increase in capabilities (at the top of [alignment](15)), the invasion of AGI on all societies assumes a wide spread of the supporting infrastructure.

**A large-scale techno-skepticism in itself can be enough to drastically slow down AGI-dominance takeoff, although that can conflict with labs’ goals of TAI.**

#### Labs do care about alignment

The relevant problem here is the inadequacy of policy evaluations. Research points to our very concept of evaluation benchmarks being flawed.[^6] Indeed the [dilemma at the core of AI safety](16.md) claims that whereas intelligence often implies a quality preserved over significant distribution shifts, benchmarking usually is not agnostic of the datasets used for testing (in a worse case, training).

**Such evaluations are viable that are very low complexity, possible to run repeatedly simultaneously with the agent under evaluation and operate with shifting alignment targets that the agent cannot learn.**

### Recapitulating the [dilemma at the core of AI safety](16.md)

#### Current state

I am writing this with the knowledge that industry is near the point of creating TAI, but not AGSI – meaning that what will qualify some entity as AGSI is a characteristic not present in current AI ecosystems, and possibly present in ones developed from them. Given the iterative, engineer-like, empirical approach to this innovation (along with poor interpretability), in the overwhelmingly likely sequence of events that characteristic will appear first and its presence will be verified later. As we do not yet have a clear, simple-to-evaluate set of criteria for what qualifies as such, we shall expect AGSI to operate unidentified for a while.

**In the following we shall focus on risks and mitigations relevant to that period.[^7]**

#### Fundamental concepts

1. *“Emergence” vs “integrity”.*   
   1. Considering the ensemble of experiments with new AI architectures, whether contemporary or as a series ordered in time,[^8] both TAI and AGSI presence is deemed emergent, since a crucial requirement from them is exhibiting global effects.   
   2. Critically, as in the period under investigation the precise nature of those effects is unknown, any estimation of indicators is grounded in the present, pre-transition-to-AGSI era and specifically its technological paradigms – which may or may not hold out post-transition.  
   3. The type of integrity assumed about the ecosystem in transition is composed of the paradigms and values expected to hold out, and it is instructive to make the assumption explicit and justified, just as importantly, we shall reason about the consequences of our assumptions being correct or not.  
2. *IQ and Goodhart's law.*  
   1. Intelligence having piqued the curiosity of quantitative scientists, the need for its measurement arose and was promptly fulfilled, at least to some degree of precision – IQ being a particularly enduring early example –, and in modern IQ testing it is required that the measure of intelligence be a robust metric of the subject, even if gradually shifting across age group, sex and cultural heritages, the least possible dependent on emotional state, physical conditions, social situation etc.  
   2. Remarkably, multiple IQ tests that evidently measure such intelligence have been created and validated to high accuracy, confirming the legitimacy of that concept.  
   3. Then intelligence (or strictly speaking, IQ) belongs to the traits that can be used to the effect of defining the subject’s identity as distinguishing them from the rest of a sample, and so transforming them into a distinct individual by changing it (if that is even possible).  
   4. Contrast that to how AI model capabilities are presumed to be instantly improved by better interfaces, scaffolding, prompting etc. – this suggests that capability benchmarks effectively test *the model interaction with the test set*, although important, not focusing on an intelligence forming the model identity.[^9]  
3. *The utility-trust tradeoff.*  
   1. Even for TAI (also past innovations), a critical mass of users need to trust the alignment of a new piece of technology for it to be sustainable, which is influenced by many sources, among them proven failure probabilities, impact and mitigation strategies – the expected outcome is weighed against the benefit of using the technology.[^10]  
   2. In case of such novelty that failure modes and impact are genuinely unknown or have low consensus, such as with TAI and AGSI, the reasoning becomes impossible, a Pascal’s wager.  
   3. An important factor in trust for adoption is the assumed community of destiny, at least at the survival level, with a crowd of subjects recognized to have adopted the invention, which may trump rational estimation or prompt the update of a subjective world model.[^11]

#### Superhuman tasks for AI (or assumptions of integrity)

1. The assumptions are of this form:  
   1. Consider a set S of postulates, paradigms, practices of human values etc.  
   2. S is true for a certain context[^12] except for a diminishingly small set of cases, especially when they are weighed by how strongly they belong to the context (edge cases).  
   3. The same context will evolve into a new (possibly identical) one post-AI-transition through transformation steps that are always salient and acknowledged.  
   4. In the new context S is still evaluated true (possibly except in edge cases).  
2. The assumptions can be incorrect (assuming context can always be narrowed down to an appropriate one)  
   1. in 3 ways:  
      1. a priori: S is currently false  
      2. about the time of transition: it has happened at the time of assumption  
      3. a posteriori: S is false post-transition  
   2. whether correct (C) or not (I) about transition timing, we shall care only about a posteriori incorrectness  
3. Case (C): based on previous writing, we assume integrity with the following subset of S:  
   1. Our interaction with AI gives us convincing narratives that we are   
      1. not being deceived or disempowered, i. e.  
      2. not losing our own epistemology or agency, i. e.  
      3. not competed with (because AI could do these to outcompete us).  
   2. Our vague instructions to AI are specified (and enacted) so that our latent needs get satisfied, which are more integral to us than the salient metrics recorded in our interaction.  
   3. Via upgrading to AI assistance we achieve more frequent occasions of such satisfaction.   
   4. The competitive culture that brings the transformation about is preserved.  
4. Case (I): we have to consider how we can quickly detect the transition and what S is highly correlated to failure of detection, thereby still holding out post-transition   
   1. In addition to S, we shall find a similarly defined set R but which *could currently frequently be evaluated and change in truth value during the transition, but correctly evaluating R will not succeed BEFORE the transition.*  
   2. Post-transition AI *will* correctly evaluate R.   
   3. Still we will propose candidates Q for R and test state of the art AI in evaluating them, which will mean using AI for a purpose it is not built for, possibly not even conceived of in design.  
   4. Notably this creative use is a practice of relaxed, or intentionally restrained, integrity – deeming it integral explicitly is paradoxical, so instead –, an implicit reinforcement, a culture (aesthetic, meme, religion etc.) must be nurturing it, let us call that “sentimentalism”.  
   5. Appreciation of and fascination with stylistically-anchored movements like sentimentalism is amplified by AI style transfer, contributing to the prevalence of the culture itself or its signature in AI applications.  
   6. Possibly we will have started using AI for improving itself before the transition.  
5. I consider the assumptions in both cases (C and I) the most fundamental, least challenged ones in the current discourse on AGSI, and as such they manifest in most use cases.

#### Best (and worst) behavior in preparation for AI transitions

1. Here i am attempting to give first intuitions on revealing and destructive processes in not following the intelligent design directions above, but downplaying their merit in favor of straightforward, market-demand-driven, “classical” AI R & D.  
2. Flaws: processes that can be expected to be detected in classical (current) R & D.  
   1. Recalling the above notion of emergence of TAI and AGSI, that was still projected in the classical community of practices, moreover, the ensembles we considered there and the human practitioners (developers, testers, users, communicators) can be thought of as connected by a single, collective interface: a connection of society to a population of model( deployment)s or to a lineage of versions, implementing plans evolving as governed by effects propagating in the same society.  
   2. More concisely, in the medium of society, the traits of AI design undergo evolution on the substrate of models (as specimens) and architectures (as species), with fitness quantifying the appeal of the collection of applications supported by the same model, conjured by  
      1. There are cultural differences enforced by psychological games and wisdom combined with charisma that also help in “status”  
      2. Memes that fall closer to the mode of, and so definitely far from peak, intelligence in humans are advantaged by being relatable, and this stands against cutting-edge AI that is still highly doubted to be such in cultural context  
      3. Benchmarks: inadvertently good indicators of agency of the model, since the better its reported performance, the likelier that people will choose it  
   3. Intelligence is not the only trait favored by evolution, and it will not be naturally aligned with it as such, instead would create eugenic selection pressure to optimize for it.  
   4. These compound to misalignment by degrading the intelligence target.  
   5. Self-preservation is a necessary instrumental goal of the specimens in evolution (the persistence of the trait being the terminal goal), in contrast to eugenic selection, i. e. In what i have called intelligent design, where it conflicts with designer intent.[^15]   
3. Lessons: processes in this evolutionary view that can inspire intelligent design.  
   1. There is a moment not yet quite explored when a cohort of specimens having evolved complex collective behavior transition to a distinct new species with its own reproductive mechanism[^16] – understanding the dynamics of this step would provide insight into AI models spontaneously gaining autonomous lifecycle control.  
   2. There is a correlation between the evolution of (roughly) increasingly complex limb anatomy, social structure and nervous system anatomy that is not yet paralleled in AI innovation, albeit an object of ongoing research.  
   3. Although we are at an early stage in AI evolution, the assumptions of integrity have surfaced in this organic kind of development, finding their origin in culture may forecast similar persistent memes in the longer-term future. 

#### Symbolism and credit

1. Interpreting policies of humans is, similarly to that of AI, a daunting task, yet what is at least to some degree made very concrete, explicit and *symbolic* is credit assignment by humans, and this is the frame of reference to markets, legislation and conflict resolution, prompting the idea that in a similar way, making at least credit assignment symbolic in future AI’s would facilitate coexistence with them.  
   1. This symbolism is partly expressed in natural language to humans, partly by means of more strictly formulated records (contracts, accounts, certificates, permits etc.), both sometimes bearing metacommunication significance that is specific, very distinct from the literal meaning and more important than it in context,[^17] whereas the context is rarely made explicit and still clearly inferred by adopters of a certain culture.  
   2. The effectiveness of adversarial input like jailbreaking prompts to LM’s or poison to visual models suggests a variety of input characteristics *unexpectedly challenging* models (and not at all humans), highlighting the lack of human metacognition on processing the concerned input, not just by humans but AI as well.  
2. Recall how representation of [concepts](10.md) is sought in AI but the current best attempt at it in humans using quantum probability theory is yet to be applied to that, in addition it is proven that the estimation of quantum states with the lowest expected error is with one that reproduces measurements to the observation level and has the highest entropy beyond that, hinting that the best conceptual interpretation of agent state may be expressed using observation levels and estimating *the maximal entropy over concepts not probed for.*  
   1. Informally that hypothesis states that the new piece of information we gain in conceptually interpreting the agent state is the most unexpected, therefore if some assumption of integrity of the *external* world model is correct, then some corresponding concept acquisition[^18] should necessitate a far-reaching update to the incomplete internal world model.  
   2. Natural language does poorly at encoding concepts but well at frames, which is what LM’s are being optimized for, and it has been shown that this produces some capabilities in predicting true statements in a formal system intended to capture concepts.[^19]  
   3. If agents have acquired different concepts (or structured them differently) and they are being conceptually interpreted, possibly by each other, in the more detail their concepts are compared, the likelier it is to find misalignment (this is an effective way to elicit human conflict, even using natural language).   
3. I argue by the above that conceptually interpreting agents in interaction illuminates the dynamics of situations where their policies lead to escalating conflict.  
   1. Flaws to interpretability

      1. While circuits with meaning anchored to commonly known operations are reassuring findings about the expressiveness of weight encoding, and tools like SAE are effectively discerning all features evidently distinguished by it, the concern is sometimes raised that not all meaningful features or circuits can be identified – the overlooked idea is that not necessarily all identified features convey meaning that is relevant to the intended application.[^20]

      2. Arguably some agent states or neural activations are more essential to be interpreted, e. g. in the process of jailbreaking we could find the location and evolution of an activation pattern that causes the misaligned output to appear, and monitoring that would enable interrupting the process.

      3. The previous argument can be applied to the process of a model individually deciding to act deceptively, the more complex a cover narrative is, the more neural activity should be necessary to create it, and the easier it would be to intercept it.[^21]  
   2. The capability of AI used in interfaces establishing otherwise verifiable human connections must coincide with high interpretability.  
      1. In particular, detailed qualitative analysis of the resulting human interaction has to reveal, moreover, *describe* the policy to the extent that it is meaningful to the users.  
      2. Meanwhile, superfluous or misaligned behavior can be detected.  
   3. This leads to conceiving of a mechanism whereby policies are extracted and stored symbolically post-training, and if enhancement by further training is needed, a loose, e. g. natural language interpretation of them (prompts) give a starting point to new policy search; some advantages to this:  
      1. the linguistic encoding can cover cultural context that evolves over time or as practitioners’ collective intelligence improves, it remains as relatable in the future as the language itself that was used at the occasion of encoding;  
      2. parts of the policy that are not recognized as effective by the practitioners yet contribute complexity do not survive to a next iteration;   
      3. illegal policies can be corrected.  
4. Credit assignment spans human economy and culture, too: e. g. status in both domains is elevated or degraded by it, and credit in both domains can be converted through that, or even directly.[^22]  
   1. Credit must mean trust, liability and responsibility.  
   2. It is a currently conspicuously unsolved challenge that different cultural bubbles use wildly different methods of credit assignment that translate very poorly, somewhat effectively via economy, or not at all.

   3. Conflict resolution without credit assignment is to the detriment of the other subject’s status.
