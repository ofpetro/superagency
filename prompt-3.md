This document is a collection of first impressions about a possible generalized characterization, or practically applicable guidelines, to critically analyze a certain type of narrative: let's call it "non-firing guns".
#### Indicators of applicability
These are circumstances along the arc that help us recognize it as it plays out, possibly earlier than we naively would.
1. the opportunity of early failure built into the predicted narrative 
	1. e. g. poor preparations, relying on false assumptions
2. different groups interpret and forward-chain reasoning about their observations differently
	1. this signals different levels of the observation accuracy (or different access to the environment) in each group
	2. less informed groups are the ones exhibiting more panic and tendency to escalate conflict
#### Social roles and structures that contribute
As all examples (seen so far) involve human characters embedded in social behavior, here we describe these characters from this perspective.
1. presence of an action-averse senior
	1. they have high status: downstream events, other people's decisions depend on them
	2. they put up resistance against automated protocols
2. low margins of security checks on propagating the projected effects
	1. i. e. each opportunity to break the expected causal chain is easily and likely overlooked or dismissed
	2. e. g. serendipitous design
	3. yet the (so far) collected examples constitute a strong track record of them working effectively against vitally dangerous outcomes
	4. a possible structural basis of this effectiveness is stable presence of veto power individuals averse to such risks
	5. another factor can be the existential worry stemming from a conviction that actions will bring about radical change, thus inevitably putting the agent into an unknown, unreliable new environment
3. detection is highly scrutinized, in multiple possible ways
	1. a highly-specified and unlikely signature of trigger events for cascading society-wide episodes is required
	2. multiple detection systems cross-check each other and hinder automated reaction
	3. either by expert or a larger selection of the public is the source of scrutiny
#### Technical aspects that contribute
Here we list possible features of some policy, infrastructure or machine design that accompany the stories.
1. deceptively communicating, especially _understating_ escalation ladders to potential adversaries
	1. this is an assurance that a weak signal of engaging in conflict from the perceived adversary is dismissed as false detection
	2. this cannot be easily directly confirmed in this kind of stories because they are defined precisely by a lack of escalation 
2. key actors or leaders, characters giving directions display
	1. skepticism
	2. commitment against power-seeking
	3. failure to repress or active amplification of signals leading to the anticlimactic narrative
3. de-escalation ladders in place
	1. protocols that help signal willingness to disengage from action
	2. opportunity of reciprocal action
	3. alternative resolution steps (to the initially suggested ones) are revealed in a timely and clear fashion
4. a sense of an opportunity window having closed
5. polarization disruption
	1. by an external 3rd actor
	2. starting with a recognition of animosity
#### Abstract population dynamics
We shall turn to more fundamental, physics, statistics and network-science-related qualitative ideas -- which could turned into falsifiable hypotheses or conjectures in the adequate field.
1. an initial macro-state of the ensemble representing a disposition against conflict
	1. individuals take simple actions with a finite number of degrees of freedom and interact weakly
	2. the idea of a macro-state describes (almost) total cooperation of in a (number of) sub-ensemble(s), e. g. group conflict or making preparations for it
	3. a sub-ensemble can be at criticality, where correlation functions are divergent and susceptibility is singular
	4. unexpected events add noise to a system at criticality
	5. the macro-state of the system is not observable on a local, low-scale level, except at criticality
2. some sub-ensembles are not at criticality
	1. there are local regions where criticality is not achieved
	2. more concretely, e. g. combat preparedness varies significantly among elements
	3. more concretely,  e. g. a series of checks and balances decelerate propagation of any decision to action
	4. unexpected events cannot be noise or a seed that triggers a phase transition if they occur in such a region
3. information propagation is made asymmetric
	1. between perpetrators of repression vs a weak majority
	2. between escalation-seeking or scheming agents and skeptics
	3. this can both defend or help dismantling the plot -- that of the schemers -- in our examples the story progresses due to this, whether it is about a failure of a plot or a failure to expose and halt it
4. systematic confidence challenges
	1. i. e. increased randomness in an agent's policy
	2. these significantly muddle course of actions in a scheme (and possibly contribute to the narrative arc)
