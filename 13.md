### Alternative to the fallible dichotomy of prediction and impact

Q: On what presumptions do we even find it useful to engage in prediction and do they set us up to fail?

A: Note that it is even unclear if there is any presumption or justification behind this.[^1] But some heavily implied frames are:

* Causal mechanisms: we can clearly distinguish if a correlation is spurious, i. e. one which would not appear were we to repeat an experiment, or causal, i. e. composed of ones that would consistently repeat in repeated experiments.  
* Sufficient certainty: of all the causal mechanisms and causes involved in them, we (could) have certainty about enough in order to also be certain about (probabilities) of consequences.  
* Independent agency: we are agents who have actions in their policies that they pick based on completely internal observations or states and as such can create impact, i. e. consequences with (at least partially) hidden causes.

If not leading directly to failure of prediction, these bar the cases when we can express or act out ideas that are not consistent with a current world model but will be after some model update is necessarily performed. If we are dealing with such a world model that is widely shared among agents other than us, this amounts to barring being informed about the future (beyond some transformation that other agents do not foresee), i. e. making better predictions (than any other agent).

**Therefore those best predict great future transformations whose predictions are irrational in view of or not grounded on the common axioms of making predictions in the first place.**

Q: How to circumvent this?

A: One way is to discard the axioms altogether.

**Instead, we can consider** 

1. **events or actions as contributing to or decelerating a conceived process**  
2. **which processes are ongoing in which phase**  
3. **recognizing and/or influencing which next phases are expected**  
4. **describing the environment in those next phases.**
